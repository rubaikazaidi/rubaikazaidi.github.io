From Soil Sensors to the Earth Prize: Building AquaSmart

How an everyday observation about water wastage became an IoT-enabled automated irrigation prototype.

By Rubaika Zaidi • 2 min read • AquaSmart

Sometimes, an engineering idea begins with a simple observation. For me, it was the amount of water wasted when plants are watered without considering actual soil conditions or rainfall.

That observation led to AquaSmart, an IoT-enabled automated irrigation system designed to make plant watering more efficient and sustainable.

Using an Arduino Uno, a soil moisture sensor, a rainfall sensor and a relay-controlled water pump, I developed a prototype that responds to changing environmental conditions. By monitoring both soil moisture and rainfall, the system determines when irrigation is needed and automatically operates the pump accordingly.

I also integrated Blynk, allowing users to remotely monitor sensor readings and control the irrigation system. Bringing together hardware, programming and IoT connectivity was an exciting learning experience, involving plenty of experimentation, calibration and troubleshooting.

AquaSmart eventually became my submission to The Earth Prize 2026, giving me an opportunity to connect my interest in technology with a larger sustainability challenge.

The project reinforced something I have come to appreciate about engineering: meaningful innovation does not always begin with a groundbreaking invention. Sometimes, it begins with noticing an everyday problem and having the curiosity to build a possible solution.
