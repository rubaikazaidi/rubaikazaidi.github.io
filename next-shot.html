The Next Shot: Lessons from 10m Rifle Shooting

On preparation, resilience and learning to trust the process when things don't go as planned.

By Rubaika Zaidi • 2 min read • 10m Air Rifle

In 10m air rifle shooting, everything comes down to a few millimetres. The placement of your feet, the pressure of your cheek against the stock, and the rhythm of your breathing can all influence a shot.

I spent nearly two years training for 18 hours a week, refining my technique and repeating the same sequence until it became instinctive. I believed preparation meant eliminating mistakes. Competition taught me that it meant learning to respond to them.

At the 68th National Shooting Championships in Bhopal, my rifle failed to fire on my first shot. Twenty-five minutes passed before it was repaired, leaving me with just 65 minutes to complete the match. Months of preparation suddenly seemed insignificant.

But I returned to what I knew: my stance, breathing, positioning, and shot execution. I stopped worrying about the lost time and focused on one shot at a time. I finished with a personal best of 625.3.

That day, I understood that preparation isn't about ensuring everything goes right. It's about building a process you can trust when it doesn't.

Whether I'm on the firing line or debugging a project, I carry the same approach: pause, reassess, return to the fundamentals, and take the next step.

Preparation cannot guarantee perfect conditions, but it gives you something to rely on when things go wrong.
