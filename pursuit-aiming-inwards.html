SPORT / 50M RIFLE SHOOTING

The Pursuit of
Aiming Inwards.

On accuracy, precision, resilience, and discovering that the greatest competition in rifle shooting is often with yourself.

PERSONAL REFLECTIONS 50M FIREARM RIFLE 4 MIN READ
← Back to all writings

For the past two years, I've balanced my high school academics with competitive rifle shooting. What began on the 10M air rifle range has expanded to the 50M event over the last six months. Recently, I crossed a major milestone by winning at the zonal championships.

In this sport, your biggest competitor is yourself. Unlike many other sports, rifle shooting demands that you outperform your own nerves. Every time you peer through the rifle sight and your eyes locate the black bullseye, your body stance is as motionless as it can be.

It is at this point that you need to go further: immobilise your thoughts, regulate your breath, and shut out your perception of the outside world. The slightest movement of the barrel can result in missing the target entirely.

As I reached the milestone of making the podium at the West Zone Shooting Championship, I increasingly realised that the real battle is with your own mind.

A rifle shooter is trained to chase accuracy. Hitting the bullseye requires absolute clarity: filtering out external chaos and aligning every breath and movement towards a singular goal.

But hitting the 10-ring once is not enough. You need to do it consistently, with precision, shot after shot, across 60 shots. That means developing muscle memory, trusting your training, and executing the same process every time. It is the discipline of turning a moment of success into a consistent standard.

Accuracy. Precision. Resilience.

And finally, comes resilience: the ability to regain composure even after dropping a point. Competitive rifle shooting has taught me to compartmentalise a setback, take a breath, reset, and focus completely on the next shot rather than allowing one mistake to dictate what comes next.

I have experienced this personally in two of my competitions. Learning to move beyond a disappointing shot and return to the process has been one of the most valuable lessons the sport has taught me.

The real challenge isn't simply hitting the bullseye. It's learning to regain your focus, trust your process, and do it all over again.

The medals are treasured rewards, but the lessons that target shooting has taught me are truly priceless.

I feel humbled and truly grateful to my coaches at Lakshya Shooting Club, my school, Avalon Heights International School, and my family for their constant encouragement, support, and guidance throughout this journey.
